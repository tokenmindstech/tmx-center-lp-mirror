# Section Library

A catalogue of section types and layout skeletons to compose a premium B2B page
from. The goal is **variety**: pick different skeletons for adjacent sections so
the page never reads as one card grid repeated down the page. Treat these as
starting points to adapt to the brief — not fixed templates.

Rule of thumb: as you lay out the page, no two adjacent sections should share the
same skeleton, and the page overall should use at least 4–5 *different* ones.

## Hero patterns

The hero is a thesis, not a template. Open with the most characteristic, business-
relevant thing in the subject's world. Avoid the default "big headline + two
buttons + floating abstract cards."

- **Split hero** — strong positioning headline + short subline + primary CTA on
  one side; a real business visual on the other (product UI composite, map,
  workflow snapshot, or an executive/real-context image slot). The visual carries
  weight equal to the headline.
- **Framed-product hero** — headline stack over/beside a large, clean product
  mockup that looks preloaded with realistic (not tiny/unreadable) content.
- **Context hero** — headline over a real-world scene or map/system backdrop that
  situates the product in its actual use context, with a subtle grid/line/map
  texture rather than pure gradient.
- **Statement hero** — oversized typographic thesis with one supporting visual
  element; use when the positioning line itself is the strongest asset.

Whatever the pattern: big readable headline, one tight subline, one clear primary
CTA (a secondary link is fine), and a visual with genuine business meaning near
the top — not a purely digital/abstract composition.

## USP / value strips

For the 3–5 things that matter most. These earn **big** treatment.

- **Big-icon USP row** — each USP: a large, thick, high-contrast icon (unique per
  item), a big headline, and one short supporting line. Not a wall of small tiles.
- **Offset alternating USPs** — stack 3–4 USPs as alternating left/right rows,
  each with its own visual. Reads richer than a flat grid and stays scannable.
- **Numbered value ladder** — only if the USPs form a real sequence; otherwise
  numbering is decoration.

Never shrink an important USP into a small header. Important = big.

## Product & UI showcase

- **Anchored UI split** — product UI composite on one side, tightly bound to 2–3
  short capability lines on the other. The visual and copy must read as one
  composition, sharing the section's background — not a heavy-treatment visual
  half next to a plain-background text half.
- **Layered mockup** — a primary product screen with one or two smaller supporting
  UI fragments (a card, a menu, a chart) layered for depth. Keep text legible;
  never spreadsheet-dense.
- **Demo/video block** — a complete, intentional-looking video/demo frame with a
  clear play affordance and a one-line caption of what it shows. Should look
  finished, not like an empty embed.

## Workflow / process

- **Horizontal workflow strip** — 3–5 steps left to right, each with a bold icon,
  a short label, connectors between them. Makes process obvious at a glance.
- **Vertical flow with rail** — steps down a connecting rail, each step pairing a
  short line with a small relevant visual. Good for deeper processes.
- **Before → After** — two states side by side showing the transformation the
  product creates.

## Comparison

- **Us-vs-alternative table** — clean columns, bold row labels, unmistakable
  yes/no or better/worse marks. Make the difference obvious; keep it honest.
- **Two-panel contrast** — "old way" panel vs "new way" panel, each self-contained,
  with contrast between them carrying the argument.

## Proof & credibility

Only real proof. If the brief supplies logos/testimonials/metrics, feature them:

- **Logo band** — real client/partner logos, evenly set, muted so they support
  rather than shout. Never fabricate a "trusted by" row.
- **Testimonial spotlight** — one strong quote given room, with attributed name/
  role, over a clean or dark panel. Better than a grid of thin quote cards.
- **Metric feature** — a few real numbers set large with short labels. Only real,
  approved figures — never invented results.

If no proof is supplied, design the slot cleanly with an honest placeholder and
say so; do not invent credibility.

## Feature deep-dives

- **Alternating feature rows** — each major feature as a full row: headline, 1–2
  short lines, one meaningful visual, alternating side per row for rhythm.
- **Bento / mixed-size grid** — a deliberate grid of *different-sized* tiles (one
  large hero tile + smaller supporting tiles), each with a bold icon or mini
  visual. This is the antidote to the uniform equal-card grid — sizes and content
  vary on purpose.

## FAQ

- **Clean accordion** — scannable questions, generous spacing, clear open/close
  affordance. Short answers. No dense text walls.
- **Two-column Q&A** — for shorter answer sets, compact and easy to scan.

## Final CTA

- **Full-width CTA anchor** — a strong closing section (often a dark anchor) with
  one clear headline, one short line, and one specific primary action. No CTA
  clutter, no vague labels.

## Dark anchor sections

Use one or two dark, full-bleed sections as visual anchors that break the rhythm
and add premium weight — commonly the final CTA, a key product moment, or a
standout stat/testimonial. On dark anchors, cards go **white** (or clearly
lighter) so they pop, icons stay high-contrast, and the dark background wraps the
entire logical section including its heading and copy.

## Background & texture treatments

Subtle, purposeful detail that adds richness without noise:

- Faint grid or line patterns, map textures, or abstract system/diagram lines
  behind a section — always low-contrast and behind the *whole* section, never a
  random patch behind only some cards.
- Reserve any texture for a clear reason (a map behind a "global" section, a grid
  behind a "system/architecture" section). If it doesn't reinforce meaning, drop
  it. Avoid overusing gradients as a stand-in for real composition.
